
# Economic Factors Definition

## Overview

Stress testing evaluates the impact of changing macroeconomic conditions on the performance of a banking loan portfolio.

Macroeconomic variables influence:

- Probability of Default (PD)
- Loss Given Default (LGD)
- Exposure at Default (EAD)
- Expected Credit Loss (ECL)
- Capital Adequacy
- Portfolio Quality

This document defines the primary economic variables used within the Banking Stress Testing Framework.

---

# Key Economic Variables

## Gross Domestic Product (GDP) Growth Rate

### Definition

GDP Growth Rate measures the percentage change in economic output over time.

### Risk Impact

A decline in GDP growth may indicate:

- Slower economic activity
- Lower consumer spending
- Reduced business investment
- Increased borrower stress

### Relationship with Credit Risk

| GDP Growth | Expected Risk Impact |
|------------|---------------------|
| High Growth | Lower Default Rates |
| Stable Growth | Stable Portfolio Performance |
| Negative Growth | Higher Default Rates |

---

## Inflation Rate

### Definition

Inflation measures the rate at which prices increase over time.

### Risk Impact

Higher inflation may:

- Reduce disposable income
- Increase operating costs
- Lower repayment capacity

### Credit Risk Implications

Higher inflation can lead to:

- Increased delinquency rates
- Increased default probability
- Higher portfolio risk

---

## Unemployment Rate

### Definition

The percentage of the labor force that is unemployed and actively seeking employment.

### Risk Impact

A rise in unemployment reduces household income and may increase repayment challenges.

### Credit Risk Implications

Higher unemployment is commonly associated with:

- Increased DPD
- Higher default rates
- Increased credit losses

---

## Benchmark Interest Rate

### Definition

The policy interest rate established by the central bank.

### Risk Impact

Changes in benchmark rates influence:

- Loan pricing
- Borrowing costs
- Deposit rates
- Consumer spending

### Credit Risk Implications

Higher interest rates may:

- Increase EMI burden
- Reduce affordability
- Increase delinquency risk

---

## Housing Price Index (HPI)

### Definition

HPI measures changes in residential property values over time.

### Risk Impact

HPI is particularly relevant for:

- Mortgage Portfolios
- Home Equity Loans
- Real Estate Finance

### Credit Risk Implications

Declining HPI may result in:

- Reduced collateral values
- Higher Loss Given Default
- Increased recovery risk

---

## Consumer Confidence Index (CCI)

### Definition

CCI measures consumer optimism regarding economic conditions.

### Risk Impact

Lower consumer confidence can reduce:

- Spending
- Borrowing
- Investment activity

### Credit Risk Implications

Sharp declines in confidence may signal:

- Economic slowdown
- Reduced repayment capacity
- Future credit stress

# Scenario Sensitivity

## Baseline Scenario

Represents expected economic conditions.

Typical Characteristics:

- Stable GDP Growth
- Moderate Inflation
- Stable Employment

---

## Mild Stress Scenario

Represents a moderate downturn.

Typical Characteristics:

- Reduced GDP Growth
- Higher Inflation
- Moderate Increase in Unemployment

---

## Severe Stress Scenario

Represents a recessionary environment.

Typical Characteristics:

- Negative GDP Growth
- High Inflation
- Elevated Unemployment
- Falling Housing Price Index
- Reduced Consumer Confidence
