Assumptions and Scenarios

Overview

Stress testing relies on a set of predefined assumptions and economic scenarios to evaluate how a loan portfolio may behave under changing macroeconomic conditions.

The objective is not to predict the future with certainty, but to assess portfolio resilience under a range of plausible economic environments.

This document outlines the major assumptions, scenario frameworks and modeling considerations used in this project.

Key Assumptions

Portfolio Stability Assumption
Unless otherwise specified, the portfolio composition is assumed to remain relatively stable during the stress testing horizon.

This includes:

Product mix
Customer segmentation
Geographic distribution
Risk segmentation
Data Quality Assumption
The framework assumes:

Source data is accurate
Portfolio balances are reconciled
Customer records are complete
Economic information is validated
Data quality controls are performed prior to model execution.

Historical Relationship Assumption
The methodology assumes that historical relationships between economic variables and portfolio performance remain broadly relevant for forecasting future outcomes.

Examples:

Higher unemployment increases default risk
Rising interest rates may increase borrower stress
Economic contraction may reduce repayment capacity
Recovery Rate Assumption
Recovery behavior is assumed to be broadly consistent with historical experience unless specific stressed adjustments are introduced.

Recovery estimates may vary by:

Product Type
Customer Segment
Collateral Type
Collection Strategy
Economic Variable Assumption
Selected economic indicators are assumed to be significant drivers of portfolio credit performance.

Key variables include:

GDP Growth Rate
Inflation Rate
Unemployment Rate
Interest Rate
Housing Price Index
Consumer Confidence Index
Forecast Horizon Assumption
Stress testing is performed over a predefined forecast horizon.

Typical examples include:

12 Months
24 Months
36 Months
The horizon selected depends on:

Regulatory requirements
Risk management objectives
Portfolio characteristics
Scenario Framework
The framework considers multiple economic scenarios.

Each scenario represents a different view of future economic conditions.

Baseline Scenario

Objective

Represents the most likely economic outlook based on current expectations.

Illustrative Assumptions

Variable	Value
GDP Growth	6%
Inflation	5%
Unemployment	4%
Interest Rate	Stable
Housing Prices	Moderate Growth
Expected Portfolio Impact
Stable credit performance
Moderate expected loss levels
Normal delinquency behavior


Mild Stress Scenario

Objective

Represents a moderate economic slowdown.

Illustrative Assumptions

Variable	Value
GDP Growth	3%
Inflation	7%
Unemployment	6%
Interest Rate	Increasing
Housing Prices	Flat
Expected Portfolio Impact
Increased delinquency rates
Rising Probability of Default (PD)
Moderate increase in Expected Credit Loss (ECL)


Severe Stress Scenario

Objective

Represents a significant economic downturn.

Illustrative Assumptions
Variable	Value
GDP Growth	-2%
Inflation	9%
Unemployment	10%
Interest Rate	Elevated
Housing Prices	Declining
Expected Portfolio Impact
Significant increase in defaults
Higher LGD estimates
Material increase in expected losses
Potential capital adequacy pressure

Pandemic-Type Scenario

Objective
Represents an unexpected systemic disruption impacting individuals, businesses and financial markets.

Illustrative Characteristics
Sharp economic contraction
Supply chain disruptions
Business closures
Income reduction
Elevated unemployment
Expected Portfolio Impact
Rapid deterioration in borrower quality
Increased credit losses
Greater portfolio volatility

Relationship with IFRS 9
IFRS 9 requires forward-looking information to be incorporated into Expected Credit Loss calculations.

The stress scenarios developed in this framework support:

Forward-looking PD estimation
Lifetime ECL calculations
Scenario-weighted loss estimation
Relationship with CECL
CECL requires institutions to estimate lifetime expected losses using reasonable and supportable forecasts.

Stress testing provides:

Economic scenarios
Credit deterioration estimates
Loss forecasting assumptions
which support CECL calculations.

Relationship with CCAR
CCAR exercises require banks to evaluate capital adequacy under adverse economic conditions.

The severe stress scenarios developed within this framework help assess:

Portfolio resilience
Capital impacts
Risk concentrations
Stress loss projections
